# Policy-Gradient, Regularized-Dynamics and Population-Based Self-Play for Imperfect-Information 2p0s Games (through Oct 2026)

Project lens: 2-player Shin Matgo with hidden hands and stock, heavy chance, go/stop decisions, about 177 masked actions, about 33 decisions per game, and a score-based zero-sum reward. Training runs on a CUDA batch simulator at about 700k games/s on one RTX 5080. The current learner is NFSP (eta=0.1) whose best response is an off-policy Monte-Carlo actor-critic trained from a 4M replay buffer with no importance correction; it shows entropy collapse and a diverging policy loss. The goal is the strongest search-free policy.

## Q1. PPO / on-policy self-play vs CFR/NFSP/PSRO: what do the 2023-2026 studies find, and which tricks matter?

### Takeaway
The largest exploitability benchmark to date (Rudolph et al., ICLR 2026) finds that plain PPO, MMD and PPG self-play with a fairly high entropy coefficient (best range 0.05-0.2) match or beat NFSP, PSRO, ESCHER (CFR-based) and R-NaD in five large games. The 2026 follow-ups (EMAgnet, NashPG, KLENT, DAGS) all keep the "PPO + regularization toward a reference policy" template and change only what that reference is. The entropy or KL coefficient is the hyperparameter that matters most.

### Cited Findings
- **Rudolph et al., "Reevaluating Policy Gradient Methods for Imperfect-Information Games"** (arXiv 2502.08938, Feb 2025; v4 May 2026; ICLR 2026). The authors hypothesize that generic PG methods such as PPO are "competitive with or superior to" FP-, DO- and CFR-based deep RL. Across more than 7,000 training runs, the largest exploitability comparison of deep RL for imperfect-information games, the FP/DO/CFR approaches "fail to outperform generic policy gradient methods." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08938); [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006833)
  - Version note: v1 reported 4 games and more than 5,600 runs; the ICLR version reports 5 games and more than 7,000 runs. — [search summary of arXiv versions](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
  - The five games, with exact exploitability computed: 2D5F Liar's Dice (15.73M infostates), 3x3 Dark Hex (6.07M), 3x3 Abrupt Dark Hex (27.33M), Phantom Tic-Tac-Toe (5.99M), Abrupt Phantom TTT (23.31M). — [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
  - Algorithms compared: MMD, PPO, PPG (generic PG); NFSP (FP); PSRO (DO); ESCHER (CFR); R-NaD. — [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
  - Results: MMD, PPO and PPG were strongest and roughly on par. R-NaD "approached" them in some cases but usually underperformed. NFSP matched them in select cases but usually underperformed. ESCHER was noncompetitive everywhere. PSRO was poor on ADH3, PTTT and APTTT, and only sporadically successful on DH3. — [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
  - Protocol: 50 configurations per game-algorithm pair, 10M steps, 3 seeds; then the top 5 configurations rerun with 10 fresh seeds. All methods use a 3x512 MLP with Adam. Totals: about 350 configurations and more than 345,000 CPU hours. — [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
  - Entropy (Appendix E): changing the entropy coefficient alone causes large swings in exploitability. The best coefficients fall between 0.05 and 0.2, above library PPO defaults of 0 to 0.01. — [arXiv v3 HTML, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
  - PPO defaults (Appendix G): lr 2.5e-4, num_steps 128, num_minibatches 4, update_epochs 4, clip 0.1, ent_coef 0.05, vf_coef 0.5, max_grad_norm 0.5. Fixed settings: num_envs=8, anneal_lr=True, gamma 0.99, GAE lambda 0.95, advantage normalization, value clipping. MMD uses the same settings plus a reverse-KL coefficient of 0.05. — [arXiv v3 HTML, appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
  - The paper's explanation for the gap: FP and DO must solve a full RL problem every iteration and can converge slowly in iteration count, and they lack last-iterate convergence, so a policy has to be extracted afterward. CFR-style model-free methods rely on high-variance importance weighting. The authors also concede the sweep may have missed strong NFSP or PSRO configurations far from their defaults or needing more than 10M steps. — [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
  - Code is in the IIG-RL-Benchmark and exp-a-spiel repositories, linked from arXiv. — [arXiv listing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08938)
- **EMAgnet** (Maidment, Lanier, McDonald, Tsang, Vinitsky, Fox, Wang, Kerr; Riot/UCI/NYU; arXiv 2606.23995, 22 Jun 2026). The method replaces PPO's uniform entropy magnet with a KL penalty toward an EMA, in parameter space, of the policy's own weights (rate tau, one update per PPO epoch). Lower exploitability than PPO-Uniform with linear or power-law annealing in most tested games: Goofspiel-4 0.031 to 0.015 (last iterate) or 0.007 (magnet); Kuhn 0.026 to 0.013; Control-Goofspiel 0.683 to 0.105 (magnet). No significant difference on Biased RPS. Gains are largest when many actions are dominated (forfeit and gridworld-control variants). The authors recommend evaluating the magnet (the EMA network), not only the last iterate. Search ranges: KL coef 0.01-32, tau 1e-5-0.1, entropy 1e-4-0.1; 700 BO samples per method. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23995v1)
- **DAGS: Data-Augmented Game Starts** (Lanier, Monette, Baldi, Fox; arXiv 2605.14379, May 2026). Episodes start from a mixture (1-beta)·root + beta·offline states, with a flag appended to observations. This speeds exploration for regularized PPO self-play in long-horizon control variants. Caveat: augmenting start states can bias the equilibrium in imperfect-information games (beta=1 produced exploitability above 0.7 in a counterexample game); flag-conditioning with beta=0.5 mitigated this. The PPO baseline used lr 2.5e-4, clip 0.1, 4 epochs, gamma 1.0, a 3x512 tanh MLP shared across seats with player id in the observation, and a batch of 8x128. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14379v1)
- **NashPG** (Yu, Liu, Canonne, Wang, Xu, Tran, Albrecht; arXiv 2510.18183, Oct 2025, v3 Aug 2026; TMLR 2026). PPO plus a fixed KL penalty (alpha=0.2) toward a reference policy, which is reset to the current policy every K inner updates (for example 10,000 inner by 25 outer). About 20 lines of difference from independent PPO in JAX. Head-to-head average payoffs: vs NFSP +0.119 (Liar's Dice), +0.163 (HUNL), +0.174 (Battleship); vs PSRO +0.153, +0.149, +0.092; vs R-NaD +0.163, +0.104, +0.107; vs MMD not significant in Liar's Dice or Battleship, +0.111 in HUNL. Performance is U-shaped in alpha. Also: "RNaD-PPO" (R-NaD with NeuRD replaced by PPO) substantially improves on large games. The paper states there is no formal guarantee for the deep version. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18183); [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18183v3)
- **KLENT** (Ota, Osa, Omura, Harada; arXiv 2602.10894, Feb 2026; ICML 2026). Search-free regularized policy optimization with a closed-form target pi' ∝ exp((Q + beta·log pi)/(alpha + beta)), combining reverse KL to the current policy (beta) and entropy (alpha). The policy is fit by cross-entropy and Q by lambda-returns. Unified hyperparameters (alpha, beta, lambda) = (0.03, 0.1, e^-1/8) across five Pgx games. Needs about 75M simulator evaluations to reach 50% average win rate, against about 300M for Gumbel AlphaZero. Ablations: removing entropy leads to entropy collapse to near zero, with Animal Shogi win rate peaking at 75% and then declining. Removing KL also degrades. Monte-Carlo or 1-step returns are worse than lambda-returns. Stability condition in normal-form games: alpha(alpha+2beta) > ||R||²/4. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10894); [arXiv v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10894v2)
- **Superhuman Generals.io** (Straka, Lisý, Schmid; arXiv 2606.23348, Jun 2026). A self-play policy-gradient loop with sparse win/loss reward, a ViT policy, top-advantage sample filtering, and an EMA of policy parameters. Uses a JAX simulator at "tens of millions of frames per second on a single GPU" and trains for 4 days on 4xH200. Ranked #1 among more than 5,000 humans and went 199-70 against the top 2 humans. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23348)
- **Mahjax** (arXiv 2605.20577, May 2026). A JAX Riichi Mahjong simulator at up to 2M (no-red) and 1M (red) steps/s on 8xA100. Agents were trained with PPO plus KL regularization after behavior-cloning pretraining, single-round only. Learning from scratch is listed as future work. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20577); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mahjax/)

### Inferences
- Rudolph et al. ran PPO with only 8 envs x 128 steps (1,024 samples per update) on CPUs. No primary study found here tests whether the entropy optimum of 0.05-0.2 transfers to batches 1000x larger. With huge batches, gradient noise drops and each PPO update moves the policy further, so the KL or entropy anchor matters more. In practice: keep clip at 0.1-0.2, cap update epochs, and monitor approximate KL per update.
- The entropy and KL coefficients only mean something relative to the reward scale (KLENT's condition uses ||R||). A score-based Matgo reward with a heavy tail (go multipliers, bak penalties) must be normalized, for example by dividing by a fixed score scale or using symlog-style value targets, before importing coefficients like 0.05.
- For a 33-step game with hidden information, a single shared network with a seat id, a uniform-magnet entropy bonus of about 0.05-0.1 (linearly annealed), plus optionally EMAgnet's EMA-KL anchor is the lowest-risk, best-evidenced recipe. Evaluate both the last iterate and the EMA magnet network.
- Opponent sampling from past checkpoints is not part of the winning recipes above (Rudolph, NashPG, EMAgnet all use pure current-policy self-play with regularization). It is still useful as a practical robustness and evaluation tool, but these papers give no evidence that it lowers exploitability.

### Gaps
- No found study measures PPO exploitability in games with heavy chance and short horizons (Matgo-like). All five Rudolph games have little or no chance; the poker results are in NashPG only (head-to-head, not exact exploitability).
- No direct evidence on very large on-policy batches (more than 1M samples per update) for regularized PPO in imperfect-information games.
- The "power-law annealing (Sokota et al., 2025)" schedule referenced by EMAgnet, (1+rho·C)^-q, could not be traced to its source paper.

## Q2. MMD, R-NaD/DeepNash, FTRL variants and last-iterate methods (2022-2026)

### Takeaway
MMD (2022/23) and R-NaD (2022) are regularized dynamics that converge in the last iterate to a regularized equilibrium (QRE or fixed point). They approach Nash by annealing the temperature or moving the magnet or reference. The 2025-2026 theory (Kalogiannis & Farina; Liu, Farina & Ozdaglar; Zhao et al.) now proves last-iterate or best-iterate convergence for regularized policy gradient in extensive-form games. MMD-in-PPO is easy; full R-NaD is hard to implement, and its deep variants underperformed PPO/MMD in Rudolph's benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- **MMD, Magnetic Mirror Descent** (Sokota, D'Orazio, Kolter, Loizou, Lanctot, Mitliagkas, Brown, Kroer; arXiv 2206.05825, Jun 2022, v4 Apr 2023). Tabular update: pi_{t+1} ∝ [pi_t · rho^{alpha·eta} · e^{eta·q_t}]^{1/(1+alpha·eta)}, with magnet rho, temperature alpha and step eta. It converges linearly in the last iterate to the alpha-QRE when eta ≤ alpha/L² (Bregman distance shrinks by (1/(1+eta·alpha))^t). Nash is approached by annealing alpha, or by keeping alpha fixed and letting the magnet trail the iterate. The authors call it the first standard RL algorithm with "empirically competitive results with CFR in tabular settings"; CFR+ was still strongest tabularly. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05825); [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.05825)
  - Deep version: RLlib PPO with the adaptive forward-KL replaced by a reverse-KL to the magnet, plus a tuned (alpha_t, eta_t) schedule. On 3x3 Abrupt Dark Hex and Phantom TTT it "substantially outperforms" NFSP and PPO in approximate exploitability (DQN best response trained for 10M steps) and in head-to-head play. In that 2022 setup, untuned PPO did not reliably reduce exploitability. — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.05825)
  - Venue: the arXiv page lists none. It is widely cited as ICLR 2023, which I did not verify here. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05825)
  - The moving-magnet variant (magnet trails the policy) converged faster than temperature annealing, but only in tabular tests. — [EMAgnet related work](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23995v1)
- **R-NaD / DeepNash** (Perolat et al., 34 authors; arXiv 2206.15378, Jun 2022; Science 2022). Model-free, no search. Reaches Stratego top-3 on Gravon (2022 and all-time), won 42 of 50 ranked human matches (84%), and beat 8 bots with win rates of 97.1-100%. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378); [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15378)
  - Three steps. (1) Reward transform: r^i − eta·log(pi^i/pi^i_reg) + eta·log(pi^-i/pi^-i_reg), with eta=0.2. (2) Dynamics: replicator dynamics on the transformed game, with a unique fixed point and a Lyapunov function that decreases exponentially. (3) Update: the fixed point becomes the new pi_reg; repeated, this gives KL to Nash approaching 0 if the Nash is unique. The deep version uses two-player V-trace over full trajectories and a NeuRD policy loss with beta=2.0 clipping, and interpolates pi_reg over Delta-m steps. — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15378)
  - Engineering: thresholding (epsilon=0.03) and discretization into 32 quanta during training, plus test-time removal of low-probability blunders. Batch of 768 full games, lr 5e-5, target averaging 0.001, about 7.21M learner steps, Sebulba Podracer with 768 TPU learner nodes and 256 actor nodes. — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15378)
  - In Rudolph et al., R-NaD (tuned: batch 256, lr 5e-5, eta 0.2, entropy schedule size 50k) "typically underperformed" PPO/MMD. — [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
- **NeuRD** (Hennes et al., arXiv 1906.00190, 2019/2020). A "one-line change to policy gradient" that bypasses the softmax Jacobian (the logit update is proportional to advantage). It reduces to Hedge in the single-state case, is equivalent to softmax CFR in the tabular sequential case, and adapts faster to nonstationarity than softmax PG (Kuhn, Leduc, Goofspiel). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00190)
- **ACH, Actor-Critic Hedge** (Fu et al., Tencent AI Lab; ICLR 2022, not NeurIPS). The actor objective becomes "minimizing a type of weighted cumulative counterfactual regret" via a neural approximation and Hedge. It is derived from neural weighted CFR with a convergence proof "under certain conditions," uses decoupled IMPALA-like acting and learning, and beat a human champion in 1-on-1 Mahjong. — [ICLR 2022 session](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/session/8354); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2022/fu2022iclr-actorcritic); [ICLR slides](https://iclr.cc/media/iclr-2022/Slides/6627.pdf)
- **Kalogiannis & Farina** (NeurIPS 2025), "Policy Gradient Methods Converge Globally in Imperfect-Information EFGs." Regularized alternating policy gradient converges in the last iterate to an approximate Nash equilibrium for perfect-recall 2p0s EFGs, under direct, softmax and NPG parametrizations. Key structure: gradient dominance in behavioral space, and the regularized utility satisfies a proximal PL condition. — [NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/2a06f4347c81f6db4f2c3ce923fd2a77-Abstract-Conference.html); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2025/kalogiannis2025neurips-policy)
- **Liu, Farina & Ozdaglar** (ICLR 2025, arXiv 2408.00751). The first provable best-iterate convergence of a policy-gradient method to a regularized Nash equilibrium in self-play. Notes that reliance on counterfactual values blocked combining earlier last-iterate methods with deep RL. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00751v2)
- **Zhao et al.** (ICML 2025). Bandit-feedback EFG learning with a negentropy regularizer weighted by a "virtual transition"; finite-time last-iterate rate of about O(k^-1/8). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhao25h.html)
- **Leal, "Steering Equilibrium Selection in Regularized Self-Play via the Reference Policy"** (arXiv 2609.19820, Sep 2026). With a uniform reference, regularized self-play selects the max-entropy Nash equilibrium (the I-projection of the reference onto the Nash set). Anchoring the reference at a target equilibrium steers selection, with median exploitability 5e-5. Selection matters only against non-equilibrium opponents. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19820)
- **KLENT** (ICML 2026) gives a looser stability condition than Sokota et al.: alpha(alpha+2beta) > ||R||²/4, versus the earlier alpha·beta > ||R||². — [arXiv v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10894v2)

### Inferences
- Practical ranking for this project. PPO with a uniform-entropy magnet (= MMD-lite) is the baseline. EMA-magnet KL (EMAgnet) or a periodically reset reference (NashPG / R-NaD-style "update step") is the step up. Full R-NaD with NeuRD and V-trace is not worth its engineering cost, given that RNaD-PPO beat RNaD-NeuRD on large games in NashPG's study and R-NaD lost to PPO in Rudolph's.
- Last-iterate methods suit this project better than average-policy methods (NFSP), because the deliverable is a single network. NFSP's reservoir-SL average policy adds a second network, a second buffer and a distillation error.
- Max-entropy equilibrium selection (Leal 2026) is mostly harmless for exploitability. To exploit weak human-like play, a reference anchored to a human or heuristic prior could shift strength against practical opponents; this is untested for card games.

### Gaps
- No found result benchmarks MMD, R-NaD or NashPG on high-chance card games with scores as rewards. DeepNash has chance only at setup; poker results (NashPG HUNL) are head-to-head only.
- ACH's exact loss (logit threshold, ratio clip) and numeric results could not be extracted; OpenReview was blocked and the slides PDF was not text-decodable.

## Q3. Fixing NFSP and the off-policy best-response learner (DQN, V-trace, PPO-on-replay, ACH, NeuRD)

### Takeaway
Original NFSP uses DQN, an off-policy learner that needs no importance ratios, as its best response. An actor-critic best response trained on a 4M-transition buffer with no correction optimizes against stale, mismatched data, which matches the observed entropy collapse and diverging policy loss. Either make the best response on-policy (a PPO best response on fresh data, or drop NFSP for regularized PPO self-play), or add truncated importance weights (V-trace/IMPALA-style) with a small, recent buffer.

### Cited Findings
- Original NFSP (Heinrich & Silver 2016): reinforcement learning uses DQN on a circular buffer, and an anticipatory parameter eta mixes the best response with the reservoir-SL average policy. — [arXiv 1603.01121](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.01121)
- Rudolph's tuned NFSP still uses a DQN best response with a replay buffer of 2e5 (max 5e5) and a reservoir of 2e6 (max 4e6), anticipatory 0.1, batch 128, learn_every 64, SL lr 0.01, RL lr 0.01, target update every 19,200, epsilon 0.06 to 0.001 over 1e7. That replay buffer is 8-20x smaller than this project's 4M, and the method still usually underperformed PPO. — [arXiv v3 HTML, Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
- A critique of NFSP: its slow convergence comes from mixing best-response and average-policy learning. The behavior policy is mostly the average policy (probability 1−eta), and "if the behavior policy and the learning policy are very different, off-policy RL algorithms cannot efficiently learn the optimal policy." — [arXiv 2208.09452](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.09452)
- IMPALA's V-trace corrects actor-learner policy lag with truncated importance weights (rho-bar, c-bar) for both value targets and the policy gradient. — [arXiv 1802.01561](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561)
- DeepNash computes V-trace over full trajectories and recomputes network states and returns exactly from whole games stored in replay, with no bootstrapping. Its NeuRD loss is clipped (beta=2). — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15378)
- ACH also runs in a decoupled, IMPALA-like actor-learner framework. — [ICLR 2022 slides](https://iclr.cc/media/iclr-2022/Slides/6627.pdf)
- The P3O authors argue that PPO is "insufficient in off-policyness" and propose a sigmoid preconditioner on the CPI objective. This is an author claim, not consensus; the general point is that importance-sampling variance grows as behavior and target policies diverge. — [arXiv 2205.10047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10047v3)
- KLENT's ablation shows the same failure mode as this project: without entropy regularization, entropy collapsed to near zero and win rate peaked and then declined. — [arXiv v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10894v2)
- NeuRD's motivation: softmax policy gradient can perform poorly even under mild nonstationarity, and NeuRD adapts faster. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00190)

### Inferences (project-specific fix list; not direct findings)
1. **Simplest and best-evidenced:** drop the NFSP best-response actor-critic and its replay. Run on-policy PPO self-play (shared network, seat id) with ent_coef about 0.05-0.1 on a normalized reward, clip 0.1-0.2, 1-4 epochs, GAE lambda about 0.95 with gamma=1 (short episodes), linear lr/entropy annealing, and optionally EMA-magnet KL. 700k games/s × 33 steps ≈ 23M decisions/s, so rollouts are not the bottleneck and replay is not needed for sample efficiency.
2. **If keeping NFSP:** replace the best-response learner with PPO trained only on fresh best-response-policy data against the mixed opponent (no 4M buffer). If replay is required, keep the buffer to a few updates' worth of data, store behavior log-probs, and use truncated ratios (V-trace, rho-bar = c-bar = 1) for both critic targets and the policy gradient. Add an entropy bonus and a KL to the behavior or previous policy, and stop updates when approximate KL exceeds a threshold.
3. Without importance correction, Monte-Carlo returns from old policies estimate a mixture policy's value. Policy-gradient terms computed with the current network's log-probs on off-policy actions are biased. Combined with no entropy floor, that plausibly explains both the collapse and the divergence.
4. The NeuRD-style logit update (advantage on logits, with logit thresholding as in DeepNash and ACH) is an option if the policy must track a nonstationary opponent mixture. It adds complexity, and the evidence in NashPG favors swapping NeuRD for PPO in large games.

### Gaps
- No primary paper found that directly benchmarks "NFSP with a PPO best response" vs "NFSP with DQN" vs "PPO self-play" in the same game.
- No quantitative guidance found on maximum safe replay age for actor-critic best responses in game settings.

## Q4. The PSRO family and fictitious-play variants: cost vs benefit on one GPU

### Takeaway
PSRO-style methods (PSRO 2017, Pipeline PSRO 2020, XDO 2021, Anytime PSRO and SP-PSRO 2022, and later efficient variants) give a population and a meta-Nash average policy, but each iteration needs a full RL best response plus meta-game evaluation. In Rudolph's controlled benchmark, deep PSRO was poor on 3 of 5 games and only sporadically successful on the 4th. For a single-GPU project aiming at one search-free network, PSRO is high cost for low expected benefit; it is mostly useful as an evaluation or exploiter harness.

### Cited Findings
- PSRO generalizes double oracle: grow a population of best responses and solve the restricted meta-game for a meta-strategy. — [Lanctot et al. 2017, arXiv 1711.00832](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)
- Pipeline PSRO parallelizes PSRO with a hierarchy of RL workers, each training against lower-level policies. It keeps convergence and gets faster with more workers. — [arXiv 2006.08555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08555); [PSRO survey summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02227v1)
- XDO mixes population strategies at every information state, not only at the root, with linear convergence in the number of infostates. — [arXiv 2103.06426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06426); [PSRO survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02227v1)
- Anytime PSRO uses a minimum-regret constrained profile so regret decreases monotonically. Self-Play PSRO adds an approximately optimal stochastic policy each iteration and often converges in a few iterations. Efficient PSRO uses an unrestricted-restricted game to avoid meta-game simulation. — [PSRO survey, arXiv 2403.02227](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02227v1); [SP-PSRO arXiv 2207.06541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06541)
- Rudolph et al.: PSRO (DQN best responses, 1,000 sims per entry, 1,000 episodes per best response) was poor on ADH3, PTTT and APTTT, and only sporadically found Nash on DH3. — [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)
- NashPG beats PSRO head-to-head by +0.153 (Liar's Dice), +0.149 (HUNL) and +0.092 (Battleship), and notes that population methods need best-response computation and policy extraction while NashPG keeps only two policies. — [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18183v3)
- A general weakness: PSRO is hard to scale and can need many iterations, since it adds pure best responses. — [PSRO survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02227v1)

### Inferences
- The final PSRO policy is a mixture over the population. Distilling it into one network adds an NFSP-like supervised step and error. With a 16 GB GPU, keeping and evaluating dozens of policies is feasible for small MLPs, but meta-game evaluation scales as N² matchups.
- Most valuable use of PSRO machinery here: periodically train a fresh PPO exploiter against the frozen main policy to approximate exploitability, a lower bound as in MMD and NashPG evaluations. Optionally keep a small league of past checkpoints for robustness testing.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025-2026 deep PSRO paper that beats regularized PPO on exact exploitability in large games. The "efficient PSRO 2023-2026" literature was only surveyed at abstract level.

## Q5. GPU-batched self-play at about 1M games/s: relevant results

### Takeaway
JAX/GPU simulators (Pgx 2023, Mahjax 2026, the Generals.io 2026 simulator) show that once simulation is on the accelerator, simple regularized PG or AlphaZero-like loops scale. Superhuman Generals.io came from 4xH200 for 4 days with a policy-gradient loop plus EMA of parameters. No study found pairs on-policy regularized PPO with exact exploitability at about 1M games/s.

### Cited Findings
- Pgx (NeurIPS 2023 D&B): JAX board-game simulators that are 10-100x faster than Python implementations on DGX-A100, "scale to thousands of simultaneous simulations," avoid CPU-GPU transfer, and demonstrate Gumbel AlphaZero training. — [NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/8f153093758af93861a74a1305dfdc18-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2303.17503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17503v3)
- KLENT (ICML 2026) uses Pgx environments; its search-free regularized PG needs about 4x fewer simulator evaluations than Gumbel AZ to reach a 50% average win rate. — [arXiv v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10894v2)
- Mahjax: 1-2M steps/s on 8xA100, with PPO+KL after BC pretraining. — [arXiv 2605.20577](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20577)
- Generals.io: a JAX simulator at "tens of millions of frames per second on a single GPU," about a 10,000x speedup over the prior simulator. A policy-gradient self-play loop with top-advantage filtering and parameter EMA on 4xH200 for about 96 hours reached #1 among more than 5,000 humans. — [arXiv 2606.23348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23348)
- DeepNash (pre-GPU-sim era) needed 768 TPU learner and 256 actor nodes with batches of 768 full games. — [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.15378)
- Rudolph's benchmark ran on CPUs (more than 345k CPU hours) with 8 envs, so its tuned values come from a small-batch regime. — [arXiv v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08938v3)

### Inferences
- At about 700k games/s (about 23M decisions/s), the RTX 5080 will be learner-bound. Spend compute on larger minibatches and more updates per sample, with KL early-stopping, rather than on replay. Exploiter and best-response evaluation runs become cheap: a fresh PPO best response against a frozen policy for about 10^8-10^9 steps is minutes of simulation.
- Parameter EMA shows up independently in EMAgnet (as a KL anchor and evaluation policy) and in Generals.io (as a stabilizer). It is a cheap addition with good cross-paper support.
- The Matgo specifics (chance-heavy, 33 steps, score reward) favor gamma=1, GAE lambda about 0.9-0.95 or lambda-returns (KLENT shows MC returns are worse than lambda-returns), reward normalization, and a critic that can model a high-variance return distribution.

### Gaps
- No public results found for PPO/MMD on a GPU-resident card-game simulator at more than 500k games/s with exploitability measured.
- No Go-Stop/Matgo-specific RL literature was found in this search scope.
